# LinkedIn Best Practices — 2026

Reference data for the linkedin-post skill. Updated August 2026.

## Character Limits

- Regular post: 3,000 characters max
- Hook (visible before "...see more"): ~210 characters
- LinkedIn articles: no practical limit (long-form)
- Comments: 1,250 characters

## Algorithm Signals (2026)

LinkedIn now uses LLM-powered semantic ranking (confirmed March 2026 engineering blog). Key changes:

1. **Saves are the top signal.** When someone saves your post, LinkedIn treats it as lasting reference value. Structure content as frameworks, checklists, or processes people want to revisit.
2. **Semantic keyword matching** replaced hashtag-based topic classification. Write naturally with relevant industry terms. Hashtags still work but limit to 3-5 specific ones.
3. **Dwell time matters.** Posts that people stop and read (not just scroll past) get boosted. Tension, specificity, and scannable formatting increase dwell time.
4. **Comments with substance** outweigh reaction counts. A post with 10 thoughtful comments beats one with 100 likes.

## Formatting That Works

- **Short paragraphs**: 1-3 sentences. Wall of text kills engagement.
- **Unicode bold** for emphasis: 𝗧𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝘀𝘁𝘆𝗹𝗲 — LinkedIn doesn't render markdown.
- **Line breaks between sections.** White space is scannable.
- **No emoji bullets.** Reads as sales content. Use plain text or Unicode bold headers.
- **Numbers and specifics** in the hook. "12,000 lines" beats "large codebase."

## Posting Schedule

- Best days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
- Best times: 8-10am local, secondary peak 12-2pm
- Frequency: 3-5 quality posts per week (quality > quantity)

## What Gets Saved (engagement driver)

- Step-by-step processes
- Checklists and frameworks
- Tool recommendations with specific use cases
- Before/after comparisons with real data
- Contrarian takes backed by evidence

## Anti-Patterns

- Starting with "In today's fast-paced world..." (instant scroll-past)
- Generic hashtags (#innovation, #leadership, #technology)
- Emoji-heavy formatting (reads as spam)
- "What do you think? Comment below!" (generic CTA)
- Humble-brag framing ("I'm humbled to announce...")
- Thread-style posts without substance in each segment
